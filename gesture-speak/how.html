A conversation for someone who cannot speak, and someone who can hear.

Silence is one screen that sits between two people. One draws in the air and is heard out loud. The other speaks and is read as text. Neither waits for the other to type.

One turn of the conversation

Blue is the person who can hear. Pink is the person who cannot speak. Each step names what does the work.

  1. They speak

    The band's microphone hears the other person and streams the sound to Deepgram, which sends the words back while they are still talking. They appear in large type on the blue side of the screen.

    Vexo band microphone, Deepgram live transcription

  2. You draw your answer

    You make a fist in front of the laptop camera and draw in the air. Opening your hand ends the drawing. Google's MediaPipe hand model follows your hand inside the page. The camera picture is never shown and never leaves the laptop: only your lines are kept.

    Laptop camera, Google MediaPipe, running in the browser

  3. The drawing becomes a sentence

    The line drawing and the last few lines of the conversation go to OpenAI. It answers with the one sentence you most likely mean as a reply. A cup, after "What can I get you?", becomes "I would like a coffee, please." If the drawing is unclear it answers nothing, and nothing is said.

    OpenAI, looking at the lines and the conversation

  4. The sentence is spoken

    ElevenLabs turns the sentence into a natural voice and the laptop plays it. The sentence also joins the conversation on screen, so both people can read back what was said.

    ElevenLabs text to speech

  5. They answer, and it goes round again

    While the page is speaking it stops listening, so it does not caption its own voice. Then the other person's reply appears, and it becomes part of what the next drawing is understood against.

    The conversation so far is the context for every reply

What each sponsor's technology does here

Vexo Open band

The wearable

Worn on the wrist of the person who cannot speak, and connected straight from the browser over Bluetooth. Its microphone is the ear of the conversation. Its motion sensor feels the wrist, which keeps a line unbroken when a fast hand blurs on camera, and reads a slow or fast shake as a quick yes or no. Its light shows what the page is doing.

Deepgram

Hearing

Live speech to text. The band sends 16 kHz Opus sound in 20 millisecond packets, and they are passed to Deepgram's streaming model as they are, with nothing decoded on the way. Words come back as first guesses and then as finished sentences.

OpenAI

Meaning

Reads a rough air drawing together with the conversation and writes the sentence the person means, in the first person. It is told to stay with what was drawn and what was said, and to stay silent when unsure.

ElevenLabs

Voice

Speaks every sentence. A phrase is fetched once and kept, so saying it again is instant.

Google MediaPipe

Seeing the hand

Finds 21 points on the hand in every camera frame, inside the browser. We measure the finger joints to tell a closed fist, which draws, from an open hand, which stops.

What stays on the laptop, and what leaves it

Stays

  • The camera picture. Only the lines you drew are ever used.
  • The band's motion data.
  • The keys for OpenAI and ElevenLabs. A small server on the laptop makes those calls, so the page never sees them.

Leaves

  • The other person's speech, to Deepgram, while captions are on. The band's light is solid blue the whole time.
  • Your line drawing and the recent conversation, to OpenAI.
  • The sentence to be spoken, to ElevenLabs.

What it does not do

  • It is not a sign language translator. It reads air drawings, a few still hand shapes, and wrist shakes.
  • It needs an internet connection for hearing, meaning and voice.
  • The band talks to one screen at a time.
